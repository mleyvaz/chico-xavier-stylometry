1. **Severity: critical — Central inferential claim is not actually tested**  
   **Quote:** “**The decisive test: is genuine control closer than baseline?**” and “All nine comparisons … agree in direction.”  
   **Problem:** The JSON reports two separate distances for each author/metric and separate permutation p-values for “psychographed vs. genuine” and “psychographed vs. baseline.” It does **not** report a permutation test, confidence interval, or p-value for the *difference*  
   \[
   d(\text{psychographed},\text{baseline})-d(\text{psychographed},\text{genuine}).
   \]
   Therefore, the paper’s decisive claim—that genuine writing is reliably closer than Xavier’s baseline—is descriptive only. The fact that each distance is separately unusual under a different random-reassignment null does not establish that their difference is statistically reliable. This is especially serious for Humberto de Campos cosine distance: 0.640 versus 0.646, an absolute difference of only 0.0062. Calling that “Yes (marginal)” is unsupported because no margin, uncertainty estimate, or comparative test is reported.

2. **Severity: critical — The manuscript overstates what the tested null hypotheses can support**  
   **Quote:** “We interpret this as evidence favoring H1 over H0” and “ruling out the strongest form of H0.”  
   **Problem:** The reported tests assess whether particular between-group distances are greater than expected under label reassignment. They do not test a psychologically or historically meaningful “subconscious mimicry” model, nor do they compare a single-author Xavier model against a poly-author model. A psychographed corpus being distant from a 1971 spoken-interview baseline is compatible with numerous alternatives: genre/register shifts, diachronic shifts, editing, transcription/OCR artifacts, source-text exposure, publication practices, and corpus-selection effects. H0 as defined in the paper is not operationalized as a fitted or simulated null model. The evidence may reject simple label exchangeability; it does not directly favor metaphysically or cognitively loaded “poly-authorship” over all plausible single-medium alternatives.

3. **Severity: critical — The “nine comparisons” are not nine independent confirmations**  
   **Quote:** “Using three independent distance families” (Abstract); “The convergent evidence across three independent feature families and nine paired comparisons favors H1.”  
   **Problem:** The paper later concedes that “the three distance metrics are derived from the same underlying texts and are therefore correlated by construction,” which contradicts the abstract’s language of “three independent distance families.” They are not independent statistical replications: all metrics are computed from the same small, genre-confounded corpora, and two of the three feature families are explicitly lexical/stylometric representations of the same texts. Further, the three author-level comparisons share the same tiny baseline corpus. “9/9 comparisons” should not be rhetorically treated as nine independent pieces of evidence.

4. **Severity: critical — Multiple comparisons are acknowledged nowhere in the statistical interpretation**  
   **Quote:** Sections 4.2–4.4, especially “significant on all three metrics” and “individually significant.”  
   **Problem:** The JSON contains at least 24 pairwise metric-level permutation tests: 3 each for André Luiz and Emmanuel, plus 6 each for Castro Alves, Cruz e Sousa, and Humberto de Campos. There are also two global PERMANOVA tests. No familywise-error or false-discovery-rate adjustment is reported, nor is there a declared primary outcome. Several nominally significant results would not survive a simple Bonferroni correction across 24 tests (threshold approximately .0021): Castro Alves stylometric p = .006 and p = .0136; Cruz e Sousa Burrows’ Δ p = .0044 and stylometric p = .0274 would fail. The manuscript’s strongest claims should rest on pre-specified tests or corrected inference, not an uncorrected collection of p-values.

5. **Severity: critical — Chunk-level permutation inference likely treats non-independent observations as independent**  
   **Quote:** “5000 label permutations” and the group-size table listing 959 chunks.  
   **Problem:** The analysis appears to permute chunk labels, while chunks were created by “sliding windows of 500–1000 words.” Overlapping or adjacent chunks from the same books are not independent observations; they share author, genre, source edition, OCR quality, topic, and often substantial text. This creates pseudoreplication and can make p-values dramatically anti-conservative, especially for Emmanuel (380 chunks), André Luiz (334), and Humberto de Campos (112). The relevant unit for a robust authorship inference is at minimum a document/book or source-work, with blocked permutations or leave-one-work-out validation. The JSON has no document-level counts or resampling results, and the draft does not acknowledge this central limitation.

6. **Severity: major — Genre confounding is not merely a caveat; it directly undermines the core comparison**  
   **Quote:** “Control and psychographed corpora are not genre-matched” and “the calibrated distance metrics in Section 4.4 … point the opposite way.”  
   **Problem:** The assertion that distance metrics are “calibrated” and therefore immune to the UMAP-observed poetry/prose separation is unsupported. Nothing in the JSON shows that the metrics have been calibrated for genre, register, period, OCR quality, or source. Worse, the baseline is conversational television speech, while much of the genuine material and psychographed material is literary prose or poetry. Thus even a finding that psychographed prose is closer to literary writing than to spoken interview language can arise from register, not authorial identity. The paper repeatedly treats genre as a limitation while continuing to use a genre-confounded baseline comparison as decisive evidence.

7. **Severity: major — The claim that Humberto de Campos is genre-matched is not supported by the provided record**  
   **Quote:** “the Humberto de Campos case (the only one with both prose-genre matching and adequate sample size).”  
   **Problem:** The JSON contains no genre labels, document metadata, prose/poetry ratios, or genre-matching analysis. The statement is therefore not traceable to the supplied statistical record. Even if both sets are prose, “prose” is too coarse: chronicles, novels, religious-historical works, interview speech, and edited posthumous texts may differ sharply in register, topic, syntax, and publication conventions.

8. **Severity: major — The paper mischaracterizes separate significance tests as “both legs” supporting a comparative result**  
   **Quote:** “For Humberto de Campos, both legs of this comparison are independently significant … making this the strongest single data point in the study.”  
   **Problem:** The genuine-control test asks whether the psychographed/control distance exceeds a random-label null; the baseline test asks whether the psychographed/baseline distance exceeds another random-label null. Both being significant does not imply that genuine is significantly closer than baseline. Indeed, Section 4.2 says the psychographed Humberto text is significantly *more distant* from genuine Humberto text than expected under reassignment on all metrics. That finding is difficult to reconcile with the paper’s promotional framing of an authorial match. The manuscript needs one directly defined contrast test, not two unrelated rejection decisions.

9. **Severity: major — The draft’s “all figures reproduced verbatim” claim is false**  
   **Quote:** “All figures cited below are reproduced verbatim from `results/statistical_summary.json`.”  
   **Problem:** Numerous reported numbers are rounded rather than reproduced verbatim. Examples include pseudo-F = 127723.2 rather than 127723.18014513912; silhouette = −0.014 rather than −0.014413964003324509; Castro Alves Burrows’ Δ p = 0.077 rather than 0.0766; Cruz e Sousa Burrows’ Δ p = 0.004 rather than 0.0044; and several distances rounded to three decimals. Rounding is acceptable if stated, but it is plainly not “verbatim.” The claim also implies that all textual methodological and substantive statements are grounded in the JSON, which they are not.

10. **Severity: major — Several factual/methodological statements are not traceable to the JSON at all**  
    **Quote:** Sections 1–3 and 4.5, including “114 turns,” “sliding windows of 500–1000 words,” “5000 label permutations,” “60 most frequent function words,” “384-dimensional sentence embeddings,” and all claims about UMAP, PCA, Yule’s K, and dependency-tree depth.  
    **Problem:** The supplied JSON reports group sizes, clustering indices, PERMANOVA outputs, and distance-test outputs. It does not contain corpus provenance, book lists, OCR sources, chunk windowing details, feature definitions, permutation count, UMAP results, PCA results, lexical-richness results, or syntactic-complexity results. Those claims may exist elsewhere in the project, but they are not verifiable against the supplied ground truth. The manuscript should not represent them as supported by `statistical_summary.json` unless the underlying files are supplied and cross-referenced.

11. **Severity: major — One explicit sample-size statement is false**  
    **Quote:** “The baseline corpus (18 chunks) is far smaller than any psychographed corpus.”  
    **Problem:** The JSON shows psychographed Castro Alves has **1** chunk and psychographed Cruz e Sousa has **3** chunks. Thus the 18-chunk baseline is larger than two psychographed groups. This is not a minor wording issue because sample-size asymmetry is central to the paper’s caveats and interpretation.

12. **Severity: major — “Confidence intervals” are invoked despite none being reported**  
    **Quote:** “The baseline corpus (18 chunks) is far smaller than any psychographed corpus, which widens its confidence intervals in every comparison below.”  
    **Problem:** The JSON provides no confidence intervals. Nor does the manuscript present bootstrap intervals, permutation intervals, standard errors for observed distances, or document-level uncertainty estimates. The statement is speculative and technically imprecise. Small baseline size may increase uncertainty, but the direction and magnitude depend on the statistic and resampling design; the manuscript has not demonstrated it.

13. **Severity: major — The p = 0 outputs are reported without a proper Monte Carlo interpretation**  
    **Quote:** “p<0.001” throughout Sections 4.1–4.3.  
    **Problem:** The JSON literally records p_value = 0.0 for many tests, while the methods claim 5,000 permutations. A Monte Carlo permutation test cannot establish an exact probability of zero; zero typically means no permuted statistic was at least as extreme as the observed statistic. With 5,000 draws, the raw-resolution limit is 1/5000 = .0002, and a standard add-one correction would report p ≤ .0002 or p = .0002, depending on implementation. Reporting p < .001 is conservative, but it conceals a potentially flawed p-value estimator and fails to report the actual number of exceedances. The paper should state the permutation p-value formula and report “0/5000 exceedances” where applicable.

14. **Severity: major — The global PERMANOVA is overinterpreted and incomplete**  
    **Quote:** “the nine author/group labels are statistically distinguishable.”  
    **Problem:** The JSON does show pseudo-F values and p_value = 0.0 for nine labels, but the inference is not specific to authorship. The labels combine author/persona, genre, source, period, publication type, OCR quality, and sample size. PERMANOVA is also sensitive to differences in multivariate dispersion, particularly with groups ranging from 1 to 380 chunks. No homogeneity-of-dispersion analysis is reported. The extraordinary pseudo-F values—127,723 and 199,818—also warrant diagnostic explanation, but the manuscript simply treats them as decisive evidence of group separation.

15. **Severity: major — The missing 959th/958th observation is not explained**  
    **Quote:** “Total: 959 chunks” versus the clustering-quality results in Section 4.1.  
    **Problem:** The JSON states 959 total chunks and PERMANOVA n_chunks = 959, but both clustering-quality calculations use n_chunks = 958. The manuscript reports the silhouette and Davies–Bouldin values without explaining why one chunk disappeared from those analyses. This may be benign—e.g., a missing embedding or undefined feature vector—but it is a reproducibility and data-integrity discrepancy that must be documented.

16. **Severity: major — The tiny Castro Alves and Cruz e Sousa samples cannot reasonably be called replications**  
    **Quote:** “Castro Alves and Cruz e Sousa replicate the same direction despite being severely underpowered, which is itself notable — nothing forced them to point the same way.”  
    **Problem:** Castro Alves has one psychographed chunk and Cruz e Sousa has three. These are not meaningful author-level replications; they may be single-document, single-extraction, or single-OCR artifacts. Moreover, the nine directional results are correlated because they reuse the same corpora and baseline and are calculated from related representations. “Nothing forced them to point the same way” is rhetorical, not statistical. It insinuates evidential weight without quantifying a valid directional null probability.

17. **Severity: major — Emmanuel and André Luiz do not “rule out” a Xavier-origin account**  
    **Quote:** “ruling out the strongest form of H0 (indistinguishability from the medium’s own conscious voice).”  
    **Problem:** At most, the JSON supports that the attributed corpora are more distant from the selected 1971 interview baseline than expected under the specific label-permutation test. It does not show that these texts are not produced by Xavier, edited under his name, or shaped by different genre/register conditions. The phrase “ruling out” is much too strong for an uncontrolled comparison between literary prose and a small spoken-interview corpus.

18. **Severity: major — The conclusion selectively suppresses the strongest contrary result**  
    **Quote:** “psychographed texts attributed to three historical authors … are consistently closer to those authors’ own genuine writing than to Chico Xavier’s documented baseline voice.”  
    **Problem:** This conclusion omits that Section 4.2 finds the psychographed texts are consistently **more distant from genuine controls than random reassignment predicts**. The paper tries to label that result “agnostic,” but it is directly relevant: the purportedly attributed texts demonstrably do not reproduce the historical writers’ surface styles under the chosen metrics. A fair conclusion would say only that the observed absolute distances to genuine controls happened to be smaller than the corresponding distances to the selected baseline, without claiming authorial affinity or H1 support absent a direct contrast test and genre controls.

19. **Severity: minor — Several numerical presentations are technically accurate but potentially misleading in precision and emphasis**  
    **Quote:** Table 4.4, especially “Humberto de Campos … Cosine … 0.640 … 0.646 … Yes (marginal).”  
    **Problem:** The rounded values are traceable to the JSON and generally correctly rounded. However, the table visually equates very different contrasts: Humberto cosine differs by 0.0062055, while Castro Alves stylometric differs by 4.6832696. Without uncertainty intervals or a common effect-size scale, the repeated “Yes” cells create a false impression of comparable evidence. The “marginal” label is ad hoc and unquantified.

20. **Severity: minor — The abstract contains inflated, formulaic significance language**  
    **Quote:** “The best-powered case … is individually significant on every metric (p<0.001 to p<0.001).”  
    **Problem:** “p<0.001 to p<0.001” is nonsensical as a range and reads like automated prose. The underlying JSON supports p = 0.0 for all three Humberto genuine-control metrics and p = 0.0/0.0002/0.0 for baseline comparisons, but the manuscript should report the actual permutation resolution or exact available values rather than a tautological range. “Best-powered” is also not demonstrated through a power analysis; it is merely the largest relevant sample.

21. **Severity: minor — Section 4.5 makes unverified visual and feature-level claims and then dismisses contradictory visualization**  
    **Quote:** “A UMAP projection … places … [groups] in one broad region” and “Yule’s K and mean dependency-tree depth … show …”  
    **Problem:** None of these outputs appear in the JSON. More importantly, the prose uses UMAP only when it is favorable to the preferred interpretation: the projection apparently shows baseline and psychographed prose together, but this is dismissed as a register confound, whereas the distance results are treated as more probative despite the same unresolved register confound. The claim that greater lexical richness or syntactic complexity contradicts “a simplified, collapsed imitation account” is a straw-man version of H0; a capable writer can shift into elaborate literary register.

22. **Severity: minor — The introduction makes substantial historical claims without sources**  
    **Quote:** “producing over 400 books he attributed to more than 100 discarnate authors” and “one of the largest attributed-multi-author corpora available.”  
    **Problem:** These claims are not in the JSON and have no citations. They may be true, but they are central framing statements in a paper dealing with a historically and culturally sensitive topic. Likewise, corpus-source claims involving archive.org, algosobre.com.br, and oconsolador.com.br need stable citations, access dates, edition identifiers, and documentation of rights and OCR processing.

23. **Severity: minor — Citation practice is incomplete and not consistently APA-compliant**  
    **Quote:** “(McInnes, Healy & Melville, 2018; cosine metric)” and the References section.  
    **Problem:** In parenthetical APA style, three authors should ordinarily be formatted “(McInnes, Healy, & Melville, 2018),” with the comma before the ampersand. The spaCy reference lacks standard publication/retrieval details, and the cited spaCy 2 paper does not by itself document the specific Portuguese model `pt_core_news_lg`. Similarly, Reimers and Gurevych (2019) documents Sentence-BERT generally, but not necessarily the particular multilingual MiniLM model named in the methods; the model card/version should be cited. The references are recognizable rather than obviously fabricated, but the model- and software-specific claims are insufficiently sourced.

24. **Severity: minor — The draft has conspicuous AI-generated-academic-prose markers**  
    **Quote:** “end-to-end computational pipeline,” “empirically tractable question,” “convergent evidence,” “decisive test,” “strongest single data point,” “important limitations,” and the repeated three-part constructions such as “lexical, syntactic, and dense semantic.”  
    **Problem:** These phrases are not inherently wrong, but in aggregate they substitute prestige language for evidential specificity. “Decisive” is especially indefensible when the decisive contrast lacks a statistical test. The recurring rule-of-three framing and promotional adjectives amplify results that are fundamentally confounded and chunk-dependent. The final parenthetical—“should be added by Maikel before submission; none is asserted here to avoid citing sources not verified in this session”—is an obvious drafting-process artifact and should never appear in a manuscript shown to co-authors, let alone submitted.

**Overall verdict:** This draft is not ready to show a human co-author as a substantive research paper, except explicitly as a rough internal outline requiring major methodological reconstruction. Its reported group counts, most displayed distances, rounded p-values, PERMANOVA values, and clustering indices broadly match the JSON, but the central H1-over-H0 conclusion is not supported by the tests actually reported. The manuscript needs a direct inferential test of genuine-versus-baseline proximity, document-level or blocked resampling, genre/register-matched controls, multiple-testing treatment, transparent Monte Carlo p-value reporting, explanation of the 958/959 discrepancy, and removal of unsupported “decisive,” “independent,” and “ruling out” language.
